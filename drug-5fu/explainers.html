Explainers · no chemistry background needed

The ideas behind the drug test, in pictures

Six short explainers. Each one is a picture and a few sentences. Read them in order, or jump to the one you need.

1 · The drug

5-fluorouracil (5-FU) is a small anticancer drug: 12 atoms, formula C₄H₃FN₂O₂. It has been used against colorectal, breast and other cancers for more than 60 years (first approved in 1962; ChEMBL CHEMBL185, PubChem CID 3385).

A molecule like this can exist in a few slightly different forms, called tautomers. We studied only the main form, called T1 (the "diketo" form, with two C=O groups). It is the form of the clinical drug and the form seen in the lab spectrum.

Why a drug? Drug discovery screens many candidate molecules by computer before any is made. A method has to give trustworthy shapes and properties, starting from rough input. A well-studied drug with a published lab spectrum is a fair first test.

The screening pipeline is context only. We did not run a screening campaign.

2 · Energy valley: what "optimizing a shape" means

Every shape of a molecule has an energy. Picture a valley: the bottom is the relaxed, natural shape. A bent shape sits higher up the slope.

Optimizing means letting the shape roll down to the bottom. The computer calculates the forces on every atom (the slope) and moves the atoms downhill, step by step.

The energy unit here is the electronvolt (eV). A start 3.1 eV up the slope is a strongly bent ring. 6.4 eV is more bent still.

3 · Why start from a bent shape?

In drug screening, thousands of molecules are drawn quickly with cheap tools, so their shapes are rough. A useful method has to cope with a rough start, not only a perfect one.

So we bent the drug on purpose and checked that every start rolls to the same bottom. This mimics the rough shapes of a screening pipeline. It is not taken from a real screening run.

Run 1 starts close to the bottom (0.11 eV up, measured from its first recorded step). Run 2 starts 3.1 eV up. Run 3 starts 6.4 eV up.

4 · A molecule is never still

Atoms are joined by bonds that behave like springs. They stretch and bend all the time. A stiff bond, like the C=O bond, vibrates fast. A soft bend vibrates slowly.

Each vibration absorbs infrared light at its own "pitch". This pitch is written as a wavenumber in cm⁻¹. Fast vibrations have high wavenumbers (around 1750 cm⁻¹ for C=O). Slow ones have low wavenumbers (a few hundred cm⁻¹).

Stable or not? At the bottom of a valley every vibration is a real back-and-forth motion. If the shape were perched on a ridge, one "vibration" would instead be a slide downhill, which shows up as an imaginary frequency. We found none.

5 · The infrared fingerprint

Drawn from the comparison table. Hover a stick for its numbers. Bar heights: computed intensity (km/mol) above, measured relative intensity below, each scaled to its own tallest band.

All the vibrations together form a pattern of peaks: a fingerprint unique to the molecule. Above the line is the fingerprint computed with Skala-1.1. Below the line is the one measured in a lab, on 5-FU molecules frozen in solid argon at 6 K (Ivanov et al., Low Temp. Phys. 43, 400, 2017).

The peaks fall in matching places, and strong peaks match strong peaks. The two tallest peaks, the C=O stretches, are within 4 cm⁻¹ of the lab values.

6 · The scale factor, honestly

Computed "harmonic" vibrations treat bonds as perfect springs. Real bonds are a little softer, so computed wavenumbers usually come out a bit high. Here the raw values are about 21 cm⁻¹ high on average, and about 46 cm⁻¹ high above 1000 cm⁻¹.

A common fix is to multiply all computed values by one number, the scale factor. We used 0.9736. After scaling, the typical error is 11 cm⁻¹ and the largest single error is 29 cm⁻¹.

Honest note: the factor was fitted on the same 25 bands it is then judged on. That makes the 11 cm⁻¹ an in-sample number. The unscaled errors are reported too.

7 · The AI agent that supervises the optimizer

The optimizer (L-BFGS) does the rolling downhill. An AI agent (a large language model) watches the run and may change one setting at a time, such as the largest allowed step. A guard checks every change and can undo it.

On the drug, the agent saw that steps were pinned at a small size while the energy kept falling, so it raised the step limit: once in Run 1, three times in Run 2 and four times in Run 3. No change had to be rolled back. The logged decisions →
