Drug test · part of the open-source Skala-1.1 workflow

A cancer drug, a bent start, and an infrared fingerprint

The computed infrared fingerprint of 5-fluorouracil is consistent with the lab measurement, even when the workflow started from a shape bent 3.1 eV out of place.

5-fluorouracil (5-FU) is an anticancer drug in clinical use for more than 60 years. We ran the same open-source workflow used for the two annulenes on this drug, unchanged. Everything behind the result is here: code, data, figures and plain-language explainers.

Reproducible results

Every number here can be recomputed from the released data

The infrared comparison (25 bands, scale factor 0.9736, 11.0 cm⁻¹ typical error, 0.86 rank correlation) is recomputed by one Python script in about two minutes. No GPU, no account and no API key are needed. The vibration figures are redrawn from the saved force files, and the full runs can be repeated with the notebooks. Run logs, agent transcripts and checksums are published.

Three questions, three answers

1

Do different starting shapes end in the same place?

Yes. A standard start and two deliberately bent starts (3.1 eV and 6.4 eV above the final energy) all end at one structure, within 0.0072 Å and 0.51 meV.

See the evidence →
2

Is that shape stable?

Yes. The vibration check finds 0 imaginary frequencies: all 30 vibrations are real, so the shape sits at the bottom of an energy valley.

See the evidence →
3

Is its infrared fingerprint consistent with the lab?

Yes. Against a measured argon-matrix spectrum, 25 bands give a typical error of 11 cm⁻¹ after one scale factor. Both C=O bands are within 4 cm⁻¹.
