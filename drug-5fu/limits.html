Limits

What this test shows, and what it does not

It shows that the open-source workflow, run unchanged, takes one drug molecule from bent starts to one stable structure, and that the harmonic infrared fingerprint computed with Skala-1.1 at that structure is consistent with a measured argon-matrix spectrum.

What we do not claim

  • No accuracy ranking. No other density functional was run on 5-FU here. Nothing on this site says Skala-1.1 is more or less accurate than any other method.
  • No general accuracy. One molecule, one form (T1), gas-phase model.
  • No speed claim. Timings are reported as facts about these runs, not as a comparison.
  • No screening campaign. The bent starts were made on purpose to mimic rough screening shapes. They do not come from a real screening pipeline.
  • No infrared from the 6.4 eV start. That start is a geometry result only (Module 1).
  • No peer-reviewed functional. Skala-1.1 is described in a preprint (arXiv:2506.14665, version 6) that has not been peer reviewed.

Limits of the infrared comparison

  • Harmonic vibrations; real bonds are anharmonic.
  • The lab reference is molecules in solid argon at 6 K, not a gas or a solution. The matrix shifts bands by a few cm⁻¹.
  • Bands are paired by frequency order, with the character of key bands checked.
  • The scale factor was fitted on the same 25 bands (in-sample).
  • The largest single error after scaling is 29 cm⁻¹, in a low-frequency mode.
  • The C=O region has Fermi resonances in the experiment; the N–H stretches are not compared.
  • The six translation and rotation modes are not exactly zero (up to 52 cm⁻¹ in size), so the lowest vibrations carry the most uncertainty.

Open gaps

  • The deformed starting structures of Runs 2 and 3 are not stored as separate files.
  • The plotting scripts for the main drug figures are not in this package yet.
  • The AI agent's outputs are not repeatable word for word.
